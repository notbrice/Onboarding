[# Pointing Control Algorithm
While in orbit, satellites depend on a pointing control system to determine the direction it is facing in space (called its "attitude"), to turn towards its target 
(e.g. Earth) and to remain on that target during observations.

## Background
Pointing control algorithms allow a satellite to remain focused on a very precise, or "fine" area by implementing a combination of magnetorquers, action wheels and gyroscopes,
a satellite (or Cubesat) works to maintain the exact 3 axis direction the body is facing in space. For this project you can assume a 1U Cubesat with diagonal inertia and a 
10 Hz control loop

## Scope
1. Allow for disturbances in your system (residual magnetic dipoles, solar pressure, etc). 
2. When researching attitude representation, be careful when trying to assess gimbal lock. 
3. Final zip must include a readme, dependancies, and code that will compile/run.
4. Orbit determination, sensor hardware and thermal effects are out of scope for this project.

## Project Deliverables
1. Research quaternions and compare with Euler angle attitude representation. Your justification matters more than which representation you choose. 
2. Provide background for the development of pointing control algorithms (e.g. when and how they were first developed and how they evolved to become "industry
   standards").
3. Research pointing control methodologies (P only, PID, PD, etc.) and explain how each one operates.
4. Provide a comparison between at LEAST 2 different methodologies. You can simulate results such as control effort, settling time, pointing stability, etc.
5. Program a pointing control algorithm in a language of your choosing (use a zip file to submit), include any simulation files you utilised as part of this project
6. create a written plan to transfer your program onto a microcontroller
