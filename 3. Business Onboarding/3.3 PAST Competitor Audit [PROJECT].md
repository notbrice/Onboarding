# PAST Competitor Audit

## Background

PAST competes for the same students, sponsors, university support, and media attention as other student teams across Western Australia and nationally. How those teams present themselves tells us a great deal about where PAST already does well and where there is room to move.

This project produces a documented, structured audit of that landscape which the Business department and PAST as a whole can act on.

Read "competitor" broadly. Alongside other CubeSat and space teams, it covers rocketry teams, motorsport teams, coding societies, and faculty-run programs chasing the same attention.

---

## Project Objectives

By the end of this project, you should aim to:

- Define what "competitor" means in PAST's context and select a set of teams to audit
- Build a consistent framework for comparing teams across branding, communications, sponsorship, and recruitment
- Audit each selected team against that framework
- Identify where PAST leads, where PAST lags, and where there is an unclaimed opportunity
- Produce clear, prioritised recommendations the Business department can act on
- Document the audit so it can be repeated in future years without starting from scratch

---

## Scope and Expectations

This project is built on research and analysis. You are expected to:

- Work from publicly available information only (websites, social media, sponsorship packs, news coverage, university pages)
- Ground every claim in something observable, such as a page, a post, or a published figure
- Compare like with like by using the same criteria for every team
- Turn observations into recommendations
- Say plainly where PAST could improve, since those findings are the most useful part of the audit

No marketing or business experience is needed. The work rewards attention to detail and structured thinking.

---

## Suggested Workflow

### 1. Define the Field
- Decide which teams and organisations count as competitors
- Consider grouping them, for example:
  - Direct competitors (other CubeSat and space teams)
  - Local competitors (other WA student teams competing for the same members and sponsors)
  - Aspirational benchmarks (large, well-established international teams)
- Justify your selection in a sentence or two per team

### 2. Build the Audit Framework
- Decide what you will assess for each team, for example:
  - Branding and visual identity (logo, colour, consistency, merch)
  - Website quality, structure, and clarity
  - Social media presence, posting frequency, and content style
  - Sponsorship approach (tiers, packs, visible sponsor logos, how sponsors are thanked)
  - Recruitment messaging and onboarding visibility
  - How technical work is communicated to non-technical audiences
  - Events, outreach, and community presence
- Use a consistent scoring or rating approach so teams can be compared side by side

### 3. Conduct the Audit
- Work through each team using the same framework
- Record evidence such as screenshots, links, and follower or engagement figures
- Note the date of collection, since these figures change

### 4. Comparative Analysis
- Compare PAST against the audited teams using the same framework
- Identify:
  - Areas where PAST is clearly ahead
  - Areas where PAST is clearly behind
  - Tactics worth adopting
  - Gaps nobody is filling that PAST could own
- Look for patterns that show up across several teams

### 5. Recommendations
- Translate findings into concrete, prioritised recommendations
- For each recommendation, note:
  - What should change
  - Why, based on audit evidence
  - Rough effort and who would own it
- Separate quick wins from longer-term strategic changes

### 6. Documentation and Handover
- Compile the audit into a single document or shared spreadsheet
- Make the framework reusable so the audit can be re-run annually
- Note where information was hard to find or unreliable

---

## Deliverables

At minimum, your documentation should include:

- A defined list of audited teams with justification for their inclusion
- A documented audit framework or scoring rubric
- A completed comparison, ideally as a table or spreadsheet, with supporting evidence
- A written analysis of PAST's position relative to those teams
- A prioritised list of recommendations for the Business department
- Notes on how and when the audit should be repeated
