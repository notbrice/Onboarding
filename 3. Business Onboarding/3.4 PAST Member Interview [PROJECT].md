# PAST Member Interview

## Background

The people building PAST are one of our strongest selling points. Prospective members want to know what it feels like to be on the team, sponsors want to see the calibre of students they would be supporting, and a person is an easier thing for the public to connect with than a subsystem.

Member spotlights already appear in newsletters and on social media. This project aims to build a repeatable format to strengthen them: a standard question set, a clear process, and a library of material that other Business projects can draw from. You will design the format and then run it to produce publishable content.

One interview can supply several channels at once, becoming a newsletter spotlight, an Instagram post, a short reel, and a quote for a sponsorship document.

---

## Project Objectives

By the end of this project, you should aim to:

- Define the purpose and audience of PAST member interviews
- Choose one of the two interview approaches described below and justify that choice in writing
- Develop a reusable interview format, including a core question set
- Plan and schedule your interviews, covering consent, logistics, and expectations
- Conduct your interviews and capture them in a usable form
- Write the material up into a draft piece
- Collect feedback from interviewees and the Business Representative
- Document the process so future members can run interviews without redesigning the format

Filming and editing are stretch goals, not part of the core project. See Deliverables for the split.

If your content is approved, it may be published on PAST's official channels.

---

## Scope and Expectations

This project covers both process design and content production. You are expected to:

- Design the format before you start interviewing
- Be respectful of interviewees' time and comfort, and get consent before recording or publishing
- Balance authenticity with PAST's tone and branding
- Represent technical work accurately, even when simplifying it for a general audience
- Take the material through to a written draft that someone else could pick up and publish

Onboarding runs for eight weeks alongside your logbook, so the core project stops at a written draft. Filming and editing sit on top of that if you have the time and interest.

No prior interviewing, writing, or editing experience is required.

## Choosing an Interview Approach

There are two ways to run this project. **You must choose one and explain why you chose it.** The written justification is a required deliverable.
### Option A: Member Focused

Interview a single member in depth, with that person as the subject.

- Suits recruitment and culture content, where a relatable individual is the draw
- Covers their background, why they joined, what they work on, and what they have gained
- Produces a member spotlight for the newsletter, a profile post, or a personal-story reel
- Works well when a member has an interesting path into the team, or a contribution that deserves recognition

### Option B: Project Focused

Interview one or more members (as a guide, one to four people) about a single project or subsystem.

- Suits sponsor, industry, and technical credibility content, where the project is the subject
- Covers what the project is, the problems the team hit, how they solved them, and where it is heading
- Produces a project deep-dive, a multi-voice reel, or a set of quotes usable across several channels
- Works well around a milestone, launch, or delivery, and lets you show how the team works together
- If you interview several people, plan how their answers will fit together into one piece

### Justifying Your Choice

Whichever option you choose, write a short justification covering:

- Why this approach suits the story you want to tell
- Why you selected these specific people or this specific project
- Which audience and channel the resulting content is aimed at
- What you would have lost by taking the other option

Do not attempt both. Discuss your choice with the Business Lead before going further.

## Suggested Workflow

Steps 1 to 7 make up the core project. Step 8 is stretch work.

### 1. Purpose and Audience
- Decide what member interviews are for, for example:
  - Recruitment and showing what membership is like
  - Sponsor and industry credibility
  - Internal recognition and team culture
- Identify which channels the content will feed (newsletter, Instagram, LinkedIn, website)
- Let the intended channel shape the format, length, and tone

### 2. Approach and Justification
- Decide between the member focused and project focused approaches
- Write your justification before you start scheduling
- Check it against the channel you identified in step 1

### 3. Format Design
- Draft a core question set suited to your chosen approach:
  - Member focused: background and why they joined, what they work on day to day, a problem they are proud of solving, what surprised them about the team, advice for someone considering joining
  - Project focused: what the project is and why it matters, each person's role in it, the hardest problem faced, what changed along the way, what happens next
- Add space for a small number of role-specific or subsystem-specific questions
- For a project focused interview, decide which questions everyone answers and which are individual, so the answers can be compared
- Decide how you will capture it: written notes, an audio recording, or video if you intend to attempt the stretch goals
- Decide on practical constraints such as target length, location, and equipment

### 4. Selection and Scheduling (Contact Business Lead to assist with selection)
- Member focused: choose someone whose story serves the audience you identified, and consider newer members as well as leads
- Project focused: choose people covering different roles or departments within the project so the perspectives differ
- Contact them with clear expectations: how long it will take, what will be asked, and where it will be published
- Confirm consent for recording, photography, and publication

### 5. Conducting Interviews
- Prepare by reading up on the interviewee's project beforehand
- Record audio where the interviewee is comfortable, otherwise take detailed notes
- Ask follow-up questions when something interesting comes up
- Note anything worth photographing or filming later

### 6. Write-Up
- Turn your notes or recording into a written draft aimed at one channel
- Trim for clarity while preserving the interviewee's voice
- For a project focused piece, weave the voices together so it reads as one story
- Check technical claims with the relevant lead

### 7. Review and Iteration
- Send the draft back to the interviewee for approval
- Share with the Business Representative for branding and tone review
- Record what worked and what did not, and refine the question set accordingly

### 8. Production and Publication (stretch)
- Film or re-record the interview with the shots you planned for
- Edit it into a reel or short video for the chosen channel
- Capture supporting photography of the member with their work
- Apply PAST branding and formatting, and prepare the piece for publication

## Deliverables

### Minimum

- A short statement of purpose and target audience for member interviews
- A written justification of your chosen approach, member focused or project focused
- A reusable question set built for that approach
- An interview plan covering who you are speaking to, in what format, where, and when
- At least one interview conducted and captured, in written or audio form
- A written draft of the piece, such as a Q&A, a profile, or a project story
- Notes on consent obtained and approvals received
- A short process guide so future members can repeat the format

Filming, editing, and publishing are not required to meet the minimum. An interview conducted over a call or in person and written up is enough.

### Stretch

- A filmed interview, edited into a reel or short video
- Additional interviews, or extra voices added to a project focused piece
- A second version of the content cut for a different channel
- Supporting photography of members with their work
- The piece taken through approval to publication

## Suggested Timeline (no stretch goals)

A rough guide for fitting the minimum into the eight weeks:

- Week 1 to 2: purpose and audience, choose your approach, write the justification, discuss it with the Business Lead
- Week 3: build the question set and interview plan
- Week 4 to 5: contact members, confirm consent, conduct the interview
- Week 6: write up the draft
- Week 7: review with the interviewee and the Business Representative
- Week 8: process guide and final documentation

## Notes

This project pairs well with the Newsletters and Social Media projects, since one interview can supply content to both. It suits members who enjoy talking to people and writing, and it is a quick way to get to know the wider team.

Some members will be uncomfortable on camera. Offer them a written or audio interview instead, as a relaxed interviewee gives better answers.
